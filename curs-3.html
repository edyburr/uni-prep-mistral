Curs 03 — Active filters: LP, HP, BP

Conspect: filtru activ trece jos · filtru activ trece sus · filtru activ trece bandă · frecvența de rezonanță · factorul Q.

Filters — generalities

  • Filters are networks that process signals in a frequency-dependent manner. The basic concept can be explained by the frequency-dependent nature of the impedance of capacitors and inductors.
  • Filters separate signals, passing those of interest and attenuating unwanted frequencies (e.g. a radio receiver passes the desired signal with gain while attenuating the rest).
  • In low-frequency applications (up to 100 kHz), passive filters are generally built with simple RC networks; at higher frequencies (above 100 kHz) they are usually made of RLC components.
  • Two distinct types: active and passive filters. Active filters contain amplifying devices to increase signal power. In a passive filter, the output amplitude is smaller than the input — passive RC filters attenuate the signal and have a gain smaller than one.

Active low-pass filter

  • The capacitor is moved from the op-amp input to the feedback circuit, in parallel with R2. The parallel C || R2 combination sets the -3 dB point as before, but allows the amplifier gain to decrease beyond the cutoff frequency.
  • At low frequencies, the capacitor reactance is much larger than R2, so the DC gain is set by the standard inverting formula −R2/R1 (e.g. = 10).
  • As frequency rises, capacitor reactance drops, reducing the impedance of the Xc || R2 combination, until Xc goes to zero. At a sufficiently high frequency the gain reaches unity (0 dB) — the amplifier effectively becomes a follower: gain equation becomes 1 + 0/R1 = 1.
  • Second-order active low-pass: add an extra RC network at the input. The frequency response is identical to first-order except the stopband slope is twice that of first-order filters. Design steps are the same.

Active high-pass filter

  • Technically, there is no real active high-pass filter — unlike passive high-pass filters which have an "infinite" maximum passband response, the maximum passband frequency response of an active high-pass is limited by the characteristics of the op-amp used.
  • A first-order active high-pass attenuates low frequencies and passes high-frequency signals. It consists purely of a passive filter section followed by a non-inverting op-amp. The frequency response is the same as the passive filter, except the amplitude is increased by the amplifier gain.
  • Higher-order active filters (3rd, 4th, 5th…) are formed by cascading first and second order filters: a 3rd-order high-pass is a cascade of a 1st and a 2nd order; a 4th-order is two 2nd-order stages. An odd order starts with a first-order filter at the input; an even order consists only of second-order filters.
  • There is no limit to the order, but as order grows, so does size — and precision decreases: the difference between the real and theoretical stopband response also grows.
  • If all frequency-determining resistors are equal (R1 = R2 = R3…) and all capacitors equal (C1 = C2 = C3…), the cutoff frequency for any filter order will be exactly the same.

Active band-pass filter

  • The cutoff frequency of the low-pass part must be greater than the cutoff of the high-pass part. The difference between the two −3 dB frequencies determines the bandwidth of the band-pass filter, while attenuating any signal outside these points.
  • Example: −3 dB points at 200 Hz and 600 Hz → BW = 600 − 200 = 400 Hz.
  • Simple implementation: cascade passive high-pass + passive low-pass stages into an amplifying circuit — produces a low-Q (wide passband) response. The first stage is the high-pass, which uses the capacitor to block any DC bias from the source. A reasonable separation between the two cutoff points is required to prevent interaction between stages; the amplifier also isolates the two stages and defines the overall voltage gain.
  • Improved design: IGMF (infinite gain multiple feedback) band-pass — based on an active filter with negative feedback, giving a high Q factor (up to 25) and an abrupt decline on each side of its center frequency. Center frequency and bandwidth are linked to R1, R2, C1, C2; output taken from the op-amp output.
